The 4 Flight Training Stages

Our FAA Part 141 approved course is divided into four sections, from your first taxi to your FAA check ride.

  1. Pre-Solo Stage

    Individual flight lessons covering the basic operation of the airplane — preflight, engine start, taxi, takeoff, climb, level flight, turns, minimum airspeed flight, stalls, landings, and emergency procedures. When your instructor feels you're ready, you'll schedule a Stage I Check with the Chief Flight Instructor or assistant — ensuring consistent training quality and giving you experience flying with someone new, which also makes the FAA check ride less intimidating. This stage ends with your first solo flight: generally three trips around the traffic pattern and three landings.

  2. Dual Cross-Country Stage

    A mix of flights with your instructor and solo practice in the pattern. You'll learn navigation through three cross-country flights (more than 50 nautical miles from your home airport) with your instructor, plus dual flights covering short and soft field landings, crosswind landings, emergency landings, night flight, and other cross-country procedures. You should complete your FAA Knowledge Test (written) before moving on to Stage III, after passing your Stage II ground and flight check.

  3. Solo Cross-Country Stage

    Three trips you'll plan and fly yourself — your instructor reviews and approves each flight plan before you go. You'll check weather and conditions, file a flight plan, and fly the route solo. You'll also fly review flights with your instructor in preparation for the flight test.

  4. Preparation for the FAA Flight Test

    Final preparation for your FAA check ride. Once you can perform every required maneuver to the FAA's Practical Test Standards and know everything required of a Private Pilot, your instructor schedules a Final Stage Check. On satisfactory completion, you're scheduled for the FAA Private Pilot check ride — typically a 2–3 hour oral exam followed by a flight check with an FAA-designated examiner. You'll receive a temporary certificate valid until your permanent certificate arrives by mail from the FAA.
