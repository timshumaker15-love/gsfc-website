Instrument Rating

For pilots ready to fly beyond VFR conditions — what it lets you do, what training involves, and what it costs.

When you've completed Private Pilot training, you'll likely want to pursue an Instrument Rating. It's a certainty if you're heading toward a flying career, but even if you plan to stay a Private Pilot, an Instrument Rating makes your flights more enjoyable and gets far more use out of your airplane.

As a non-instrument-rated Private Pilot, you can only fly in VFR conditions — a minimum of 3 miles visibility and a 1,000-foot ceiling to depart, plus good visibility along your route and at your destination. As an instrument pilot, you can file an instrument flight plan, take off in limited visibility and low ceilings, fly through clouds for the entire trip, and land at your destination even in poor weather.

Earning the rating requires additional flying with your instructor to learn IFR skills safely, plus passing both an FAA Knowledge Test and a Flight Test. Most pilots find the process interesting, challenging, and rewarding — there's little like climbing into the clouds and breaking out on top into a bright blue sky.

Course Overview

  • ~40 hours of flight training with an Instrument Flight Instructor in an IFR-certified aircraft
  • Additional FAA hourly minimums apply
  • Completed in as little as a few weeks, or spread over several months
  • Required step toward a Commercial Certificate
